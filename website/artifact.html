    Agreement

    Two annotators, working apart, agreed on 51 of 60 reviews

    Most reviews are positive, so two people could agree often by luck. Cohen's kappa removes that chance agreement.

    Findings

    What the labels show

    Shortcuts overcount positive reviews

    Our reading finds 116 negative reviews. The dataset's lexicon labels find 99, because "not good" still contains "good".

    PositiveNeutralNegative

    Three stars rarely means neutral

    Only 12 of 60 three-star reviews read as neutral. At one and five stars, rating and text agree almost every time.

    Durability is where products fail

    22 of 28 durability mentions are negative, usually a failure within days or months. Value for money is almost always praised.

    PositiveNeutralNegative

    "Good" hides in negative reviews

    It is the second most common word in negative reviews, almost always as "not good". Word counts without negation confuse the two labels.

    Unhappy customers write more

    Negative reviews average 17.4 words against 15.4 for positive ones, and have the longest tail. Each dot is a review: hover to read it, click to find it in the list.

    110 reviews need extra care

    Mixed opinions, Hinglish, typos and sarcasm. These are the reviews automatic models get wrong, so a test set should over-represent them.

    Readiness

    Ready to test models, thin on Neutral

    Consistent labels on real text make a good evaluation set for sentiment and aspect models. At 300 reviews with 8% Neutral, it is too small and imbalanced to train one from scratch.
